How the model works

Why these four factors

  • TVL × 0.30 — the size of the prize. A token usually launches at a fraction of the value already locked in the protocol, never at the whole TVL; 0.30 is a conservative haircut that keeps the board from claiming a token is worth everything locked in it.
  • Growth — seven-day TVL change, clamped to −50%…+150%, weighted 0.60. Momentum is real, but one hot week should not decide a rank: the clamp stops a single week's spike from swamping TVL, and the 0.60 weight means growth can cut the claim by 30% at worst or nearly double it at best — it moves the result without owning it.
  • Maturity — days since DefiLlama began tracking, up to four years (1,460 days), on a floor of 0.55. A protocol that has run four years with no token gets the full 1.00: the longest backlog of value to distribute. A brand-new protocol still gets 0.55, never zero — a new protocol can still launch, it just has no backlog yet. A protocol whose listing date is unknown is scored at 365 days (0.66), a neutral middle: neither penalised as brand-new nor credited as four years old.
  • Breadth — +3% per chain, up to six chains (at most ×1.18). Value spread across chains is harder to move and easier to keep; the cap stops a protocol listed on twenty chains from gaming the rank.

Why the board leaves some protocols out

  • A wrapped, staked or bridged product of a token that already trades is not a launch. WBTC, Binance staked ETH, the Coinbase bridge: their value is already priced by the market, and ranking them as launches would put already-priced value at the top of the board and send farmers after a token that exists. This is the watchlist's own rule, applied to every early row too — without it the top of the board would be products of tokens that already trade.
  • Exchanges are left out for the same reason: their tokens trade, or they never issue one.
  • A name ending in a version (V2, V3, 2.0) is a pool of another protocol, not a protocol of its own.
  • The 24 live-token protocols are shown apart, at the market's own value, so a reader can hold a model's claim next to what a launched token is actually worth.

What the board is not

  • It does not predict whether or when a token is issued.
  • It never ranks a protocol by name: the same function scores every row.
  • Wrapped, staked and bridged products of tokens that already trade are left out, as are exchanges and versioned pools of another protocol.
  • Farm steps are a starting plan, not financial advice. Never pay to claim an airdrop, and claim only from a link the protocol itself publishes.
