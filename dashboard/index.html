How the model works

Why these pieces, and what was tested and not used

    How a farm plan is priced

      Why the board leaves some protocols out

      • A wrapped, staked or bridged product of a token that already trades is not a launch. WBTC, Binance staked ETH, the Coinbase bridge: their value is already priced by the market, and ranking them as launches would put already-priced value at the top of the board and send farmers after a token that exists. This is the watchlist's own rule, applied to every early row too.
      • Exchanges are left out for the same reason: their tokens trade, or they never issue one.
      • A name ending in a version (V2, V3, 2.0) is a pool of another protocol, not a protocol of its own.
      • Protocols that record a token sit apart, at the market's own value — 92 of them price the curve, so a reader can hold a model's claim next to what a launched token is actually worth.

      What the board is not

      • It does not predict whether or when a token is issued. Token odds are the peers' record, not a forecast: no row here says a token will launch.
      • It never ranks a protocol by name: the same function scores every row, and the curve and the odds are fitted on the snapshot, not written by hand.
      • Farm steps are a starting plan, not financial advice. Never pay to claim an airdrop, and claim only from a link the protocol itself publishes.
      • Gas is a planning estimate per transaction on the protocol's first chain, not a quote; the deposit, drop share and horizon are planning assumptions the reader can change above.
